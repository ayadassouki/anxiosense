KB_FILE: shared_symptoms.txt
PURPOSE: Symptoms that appear in both anxiety and depressive presentations
under ICD-11, with notes on how presentation typically differs. This file
is the primary differentiation tool for symptoms that look identical on
the surface but arise from different mechanisms.
SOURCE: Synthesized from ICD-11 6B00 and 6A70 descriptions, WHO (2025-01),
used under CC BY-ND 3.0 IGO.
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_REMINDER: Overlap between categories is expected and common.
A user presenting shared symptoms does not require forcing a single
category; "mixed" or "unclear" is a valid and often more honest output.

---

CHUNK_ID: SHARED-001
SYMPTOM: Sleep Disturbance
SOURCE_TYPE: differentiation_guidance
DESCRIPTION: Disruption to normal sleep, present in both anxiety and
depressive presentations.
ANXIETY_PRESENTATION: Typically initial insomnia — difficulty falling
asleep due to racing thoughts or worry at bedtime.
DEPRESSION_PRESENTATION: Typically early-morning waking (terminal
insomnia) or hypersomnia (sleeping excessively, still feeling unrested).
RETRIEVAL_NOTE: When a claim mentions sleep disturbance, retrieve this
chunk and check which sub-pattern (onset vs. early-waking vs. hypersomnia)
the user described, if specified.

---

CHUNK_ID: SHARED-002
SYMPTOM: Concentration Difficulty
SOURCE_TYPE: differentiation_guidance
DESCRIPTION: Reduced ability to focus or make decisions, present in both
anxiety and depressive presentations.
ANXIETY_PRESENTATION: Driven by attention being pulled toward worry or
threat-monitoring; the person is "too busy thinking about problems" to
focus.
DEPRESSION_PRESENTATION: Driven by mental slowing, low motivation, or
reduced cognitive energy; the person describes thinking as "foggy" or
"slow" rather than "distracted."
RETRIEVAL_NOTE: The user's own description of *why* focus is difficult
(worry vs. low energy/motivation) is the key differentiator here, more
than the symptom itself.

---

CHUNK_ID: SHARED-003
SYMPTOM: Irritability
SOURCE_TYPE: differentiation_guidance
DESCRIPTION: Low frustration tolerance or persistent edgy mood, common to
both anxiety and depression and especially prevalent in adolescents and
young adults presenting with either condition.
ANXIETY_PRESENTATION: Often linked to nervous-system exhaustion from
sustained worry or hyperarousal.
DEPRESSION_PRESENTATION: Often a feature of low mood itself, and can be a
more prominent presentation than sadness in younger people ("irritable
mood" is recognized as a valid presentation of low mood in younger
populations).
RETRIEVAL_NOTE: Irritability alone has low differentiating value; treat as
a supporting signal, not a primary one.

---

CHUNK_ID: SHARED-004
SYMPTOM: Fatigue / Low Energy
SOURCE_TYPE: differentiation_guidance
DESCRIPTION: Reduced energy or persistent tiredness, common to both
categories and to many non-psychiatric conditions.
ANXIETY_PRESENTATION: Often described as "wired but tired" — exhausted yet
unable to relax or switch off.
DEPRESSION_PRESENTATION: Often described as heavy, flat fatigue without
the accompanying restlessness — low drive rather than restless exhaustion.
RETRIEVAL_NOTE: Fatigue alone is a weak discriminator; always pair with
at least one more specific indicator (e.g., anhedonia for depression,
autonomic arousal for anxiety) before drawing any differentiation
conclusion.
