KB_FILE: anxiety_vs_depression.txt
PURPOSE: Direct differentiation guidance for the Validation Agent's
session-level differentiation assessment. Retrieved once per session
(not per-claim) whenever both anxiety- and depression-category claims are
present in a session.
SOURCE: Synthesized from ICD-11 6B00 / 6A70 descriptions, WHO (2025-01),
and the ICD-11 category "6A73 Mixed Depressive and Anxiety Disorder."
Used under CC BY-ND 3.0 IGO.
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_REMINDER: This file exists to support honest, hedged
differentiation in the report — never to force a single label onto a
mixed or ambiguous presentation.

---

CHUNK_ID: DIFF-001
SECTION: Features More Strongly Associated With Anxiety
SOURCE_TYPE: differentiation_guidance
CONTENT:
- Autonomic/physical arousal: racing heart, sweating, trembling,
  gastrointestinal discomfort.
- Future-oriented cognitive content: worry, apprehension, catastrophic
  anticipation of events that have not happened yet.
- Specific behavioural avoidance tied to identifiable triggers or
  situations.
- Initial insomnia (difficulty falling asleep due to racing thoughts).
- Muscle tension and motor restlessness rather than slowing.

---

CHUNK_ID: DIFF-002
SECTION: Features More Strongly Associated With Depression
SOURCE_TYPE: differentiation_guidance
CONTENT:
- Anhedonia: a pervasive, marked loss of interest or pleasure across most
  activities — considered one of the most specific discriminators.
- Past/present-oriented cognitive content: hopelessness, worthlessness,
  guilt, themes of loss or failure rather than anticipated threat.
- Psychomotor slowing (retardation): slowed speech, movement, or thinking.
- Early-morning waking or hypersomnia.
- Pervasive low mood rather than situational worry.

---

CHUNK_ID: DIFF-003
SECTION: Features Common to Both (Low Differentiating Value Alone)
SOURCE_TYPE: differentiation_guidance
CONTENT:
- Fatigue or low energy.
- Concentration or decision-making difficulty.
- Irritability.
- General distress and some degree of functional impairment.
These should not be used alone to assign a primary lean; always pair with
at least one indicator from DIFF-001 or DIFF-002.

---

CHUNK_ID: DIFF-004
SECTION: Difficult Differentiation Situations
SOURCE_TYPE: differentiation_guidance
CONTENT:
- Mixed Depressive and Anxiety Presentation: ICD-11 explicitly recognises
  presentations where both anxious and depressive symptoms are present
  together without either independently reaching a full, distinct
  threshold. When a user's claims include clear signals from both
  DIFF-001 and DIFF-002, the appropriate output is "mixed" — not a forced
  choice between the two.
- Agitated Depression: Severe psychomotor agitation, pacing, or racing
  thoughts can occur within a depressive presentation and closely
  resemble acute anxiety. Presence of agitation alone should not
  automatically be coded as an anxiety signal if clear depressive features
  (anhedonia, hopelessness) dominate.
- Chronic Anxiety Exhaustion: Long-standing anxiety can lead to secondary
  fatigue, withdrawal, and low mood that superficially resembles primary
  depression. Consider the apparent sequence the user describes (e.g.,
  "I've been anxious for months and now I just feel drained") as
  contextual information, not as proof of either category.

---

CHUNK_ID: DIFF-005
SECTION: Reporting Guidance for the Differentiation Output
SOURCE_TYPE: differentiation_guidance
CONTENT: The differentiation assessment should report a primary lean of
"anxiety", "depression", "mixed", or "unclear" — "unclear" is valid and
should be used when available claims are dominated by DIFF-003 (shared,
low-specificity) signals without a clear DIFF-001 or DIFF-002 anchor. Every
lean must cite the specific chunk(s) that support it, never asserted
without citation.
