KB_FILE: anxiety_indicators.txt
PURPOSE: Anxiety-specific clinical indicators for retrieval and claim-evidence
matching. Each entry is a self-contained chunk. Source: World Health
Organization, ICD-11 (2025-01 release), category 6B00 Generalised Anxiety
Disorder and related anxiety/fear-related disorders. Used under CC BY-ND 3.0
IGO (WHO ICD-11 license: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/3.0/igo/).
Wording below is lightly adapted for chunking; core clinical content is the
WHO description, not paraphrased into different claims.
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_REMINDER: Every entry below describes a symptom pattern, not
a diagnosis. Presence of one or more of these features does not establish
that a disorder is present. Duration, pervasiveness, functional impact, and
exclusion of other causes all require professional clinical assessment.

---

CHUNK_ID: ANX-001
INDICATOR: Excessive Worry / Apprehensive Expectation
SOURCE: ICD-11 6B00, WHO (2025-01)
SOURCE_TYPE: clinical_description
DESCRIPTION: Marked symptoms of anxiety persisting for at least several
months, occurring more days than not, manifested as either general
apprehension ("free-floating anxiety") or excessive worry focused on
multiple everyday events — most often concerning family, health, finances,
school, or work.
PLAIN_LANGUAGE_EXAMPLES: "I can't stop worrying about things that probably
won't even happen." "My mind keeps jumping to worst-case scenarios about
school or money."
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_NOTE: Occasional worry about real stressors is common and
expected. This indicator describes worry that is pervasive across multiple
life areas and persists over months, not situational concern about a single
upcoming event.

---

CHUNK_ID: ANX-002
INDICATOR: Autonomic / Sympathetic Over-Activity
SOURCE: ICD-11 6B00, WHO (2025-01)
SOURCE_TYPE: clinical_description
DESCRIPTION: Physical symptoms driven by sympathetic nervous system
activation, including a racing or pounding heart, sweating, trembling,
dry mouth, dizziness, or gastrointestinal discomfort, often occurring
alongside anxious thoughts.
PLAIN_LANGUAGE_EXAMPLES: "My heart starts pounding for no clear reason."
"I get dizzy and sweaty when I think about it."
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_NOTE: These physical sensations have many possible causes,
including medical conditions unrelated to anxiety. Persistent or severe
physical symptoms warrant evaluation by a physician, not assumption that
anxiety is the cause.

---

CHUNK_ID: ANX-003
INDICATOR: Muscle Tension and Motor Restlessness
SOURCE: ICD-11 6B00, WHO (2025-01)
SOURCE_TYPE: clinical_description
DESCRIPTION: Chronic muscular tension, an inability to relax, feeling
"keyed up" or on edge, or motor restlessness such as pacing or fidgeting.
PLAIN_LANGUAGE_EXAMPLES: "My shoulders are always tight." "I can't sit
still, I have to keep moving."
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_NOTE: Differentiates generalized anxiety presentations from
purely cognitive worry; physical tension alone is common and not, by
itself, indicative of a disorder.

---

CHUNK_ID: ANX-004
INDICATOR: Concentration Difficulty (Anxiety Presentation)
SOURCE: ICD-11 6B00, WHO (2025-01)
SOURCE_TYPE: clinical_description
DESCRIPTION: Difficulty maintaining concentration, often because attention
is repeatedly pulled toward worry, threat-monitoring, or anticipated
problems rather than the task at hand.
PLAIN_LANGUAGE_EXAMPLES: "I read the same paragraph five times because my
mind keeps wandering to what could go wrong." "I can't focus in class
because I'm thinking about everything else."
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_NOTE: See shared_symptoms.txt for how this differs in
presentation from concentration difficulty associated with depression.

---

CHUNK_ID: ANX-005
INDICATOR: Irritability (Anxiety Presentation)
SOURCE: ICD-11 6B00, WHO (2025-01)
SOURCE_TYPE: clinical_description
DESCRIPTION: Low frustration tolerance or a persistent edgy, on-edge mood,
commonly arising from chronic nervous system activation and the strain of
ongoing worry.
PLAIN_LANGUAGE_EXAMPLES: "I snap at people more than I used to." "Small
things set me off lately."
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_NOTE: Irritability is a non-specific symptom shared with
depression (see shared_symptoms.txt) and many other states, including
ordinary stress and sleep deprivation.

---

CHUNK_ID: ANX-006
INDICATOR: Sleep Disturbance (Anxiety Presentation)
SOURCE: ICD-11 6B00, WHO (2025-01)
SOURCE_TYPE: clinical_description
DESCRIPTION: Difficulty falling asleep due to racing thoughts or worry at
bedtime (initial insomnia), distinct in pattern from the early-morning
waking more often associated with depressive presentations.
PLAIN_LANGUAGE_EXAMPLES: "I lie awake for hours because my brain won't
stop." "I can't fall asleep because I keep thinking about tomorrow."
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_NOTE: See anxiety_vs_depression.txt for the full
differentiation between anxiety-type and depression-type sleep disturbance.

---

CHUNK_ID: ANX-007
INDICATOR: Behavioural Avoidance
SOURCE: ICD-11, WHO (2025-01), anxiety/fear-related disorders group
SOURCE_TYPE: clinical_description
DESCRIPTION: Active avoidance of situations, places, people, or thoughts
that trigger anxiety or fear, to a degree that restricts daily functioning
(e.g., avoiding classes, social events, or specific places).
PLAIN_LANGUAGE_EXAMPLES: "I've stopped going to lectures because I can't
handle being around that many people." "I avoid speaking up in meetings."
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_NOTE: Useful for assessing functional impairment and
relevant to referral_guidelines.txt tiering; avoidance of one specific,
identifiable trigger differs from pervasive avoidance across many contexts.

---

CHUNK_ID: ANX-008
INDICATOR: Fear of Negative Social Evaluation
SOURCE: ICD-11, WHO (2025-01), Social Anxiety Disorder (6B04)
SOURCE_TYPE: clinical_description
DESCRIPTION: Marked and persistent fear or anxiety about one or more social
situations in which the person may be scrutinised by others, driven by
fear of negative evaluation, embarrassment, or rejection.
PLAIN_LANGUAGE_EXAMPLES: "I freeze up when I have to talk to people I don't
know well." "I'm terrified of saying something stupid in front of others."
NON_DIAGNOSTIC_NOTE: Distinguishing situational social discomfort (common
and not pathological) from pervasive, impairing social fear requires
professional assessment.
